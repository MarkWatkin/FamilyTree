He was baptized on 07/02/1797 in Butley, Suffolk, England. John resided on 06/06/1841 in Butley, Suffolk, England. He was a Agricultural Labourer on 06/06/1841 in Butley, Suffolk, England. He resided on 30/03/1851 in Butley, Suffolk, England. On 30/03/1851 John was a Agricultural Labourer. On 04/06/1860 he was a Farm Bailiff. On 07/04/1861 he resided in Butley, Suffolk, England. John was a Agricultural Labourer on 07/04/1861. On 02/04/1871 he resided in Butley, Suffolk, England. On 02/04/1871 he was a Agricultural Labourer in Butley, Suffolk, England.

Charlotte CLARK was born in 1798 in Butley, Suffolk, England. She died in 1876 in Butley, Suffolk, England aged about 78. Charlotte was buried in Butley, Suffolk, England.

On 06/06/1841 she resided in Butley, Suffolk, England. On 30/03/1851 Charlotte resided in Butley, Suffolk, England. On 07/04/1861 she resided in Butley, Suffolk, England. Charlotte resided on 02/04/1871 in Butley, Suffolk, England.

On 26/12/1820 John married Charlotte. 6 sons and one daughter given.

Notes

1. John Richardson is born in Butley where he lives out his days as an agricultural labourer. Although James marriage cert lists him as a farm bailiff. Perhaps this is an exaggeration driven by his new wife's father's similar status, as John lists himself as a labourer through all census records before and after their marriage.; [Note Record]
2. James Richardson is born in Butley between 1834 and 1836, where he was christened in 1836. While his wife Betsy is also from Suffolk it woudl seem from her family movements that she may have made her own way to Shropshire with her family. Certainly she independently moved to near Manchester with her family. A cousin deccended from John Winter Richardson was told a story that a wealthy Shropshire landowner bought horses in Suffolk and commisioned James Richardson to "walk" them to Shropshire and to stay on and train the locals to handle the horses. Whether or not this story is true, he certainly moved to Shropshire, worked there as a groom and became a farmer of large farms in the area, having come from farm labouring parents. He first farmed at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers. He then moved to another farm, and his 2 sons took ovr Trehowel and Tyn-y- Celyn both employing staff. Is has been reported that he "disowned" his youngest, Charlotte, when he became pregnant and later married Edward Lewis (who she may have met at the blacksmth in neighbouring St Martins where he was working as an apprentice). As a widower he retired and moved back in to Tyn-y-Celyin with his eldest son. When he dies he left £630 8s 6d to sons George and John.; [Note Record]
