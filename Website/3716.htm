Notes

1. Born and raised in Maesbury in Shropshire. Edward left home as a teenager and I believe I have found him aged 17 in the 1891 census, working as an apprentice blacksmith and living with a blacksmith family of John Thomas next to the vicarage and Trevor Cottage in St Martins. This is presumably where he met his wife Charlotte Richardson who grew up on a farm in the neighbouring village of Weston Rhyn. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although in later life Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they are living in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close) A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. In 1914 Edward joins the army, although I have found no further information about this. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte dies and for some unknown reason leaves some substantial cash in her will to her brother who at this stage is a farm owner. Edward stays on at Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen after William Owen and Mary Helen started their family there and at one point even shared a room and bed with his grandson Edward Henry.; [Note Record]
2. George Lewis was born in Maesbury. Prior to his marriage in 1873 he is listed as a farm labourer still in Maesbury. He lived in Maesbury and Maesbury Marsh all his life although for some years he did live down the road in the famous St Winifred's Wellhouse, Woolston (after Edward moved out). By 1911 he had moved back to Maesbury with his wife younger children and his aged parents in law.; [Note Record]
3. David was born in Welshpool. He presumably married some time around 1837 although cannot find a marriage cert. He moved to West Felton where is wife Mary came from. He works all his life as an agricultural labourer around Maesbury area until he moved to Oswestry where he lived with his grand daughter Fanny Morgan and a grandson Thomas Curry.; [Note Record]
4. Andrew Currie was born in Stewarton, East Ayrshire in Scotland where he was christened some months later. He moved to Daventry in Northamptonshire in the 1840s where he met his future wife Rebecca Powell Adams. He worked as various jobs over his life (Tea trader, rag and bone man, pedlar, insurance agent). By 1861 he had moved to Shropshire with his family where he lived out the rest of his life. In his 80s he and his wife moved in with his daughter Ann with her husband George Lewis an their children in Munslow Cottages, Maesbury Marsh.; [Note Record]
5. James Richardson is born in Butley between 1834 and 1836, where he was christened in 1836. While his wife Betsy is also from Suffolk it woudl seem from her family movements that she may have made her own way to Shropshire with her family. Certainly she independently moved to near Manchester with her family. A cousin deccended from John Winter Richardson was told a story that a wealthy Shropshire landowner bought horses in Suffolk and commisioned James Richardson to "walk" them to Shropshire and to stay on and train the locals to handle the horses. Whether or not this story is true, he certainly moved to Shropshire, worked there as a groom and became a farmer of large farms in the area, having come from farm labouring parents. He first farmed at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers. He then moved to another farm, and his 2 sons took ovr Trehowel and Tyn-y- Celyn both employing staff. Is has been reported that he "disowned" his youngest, Charlotte, when he became pregnant and later married Edward Lewis (who she may have met at the blacksmth in neighbouring St Martins where he was working as an apprentice). As a widower he retired and moved back in to Tyn-y-Celyin with his eldest son. When he dies he left £630 8s 6d to sons George and John.; [Note Record]
6. John Richardson is born in Butley where he lives out his days as an agricultural labourer. Although James marriage cert lists him as a farm bailiff. Perhaps this is an exaggeration driven by his new wife's father's similar status, as John lists himself as a labourer through all census records before and after their marriage.; [Note Record]
7. I have reason to believe that this Samuel and wife Susan are the same Samuel/Susan that appear in a genealogical study "William D Clutton, Genealogy and family history of William Clutton (1675-1732) of Fressingfield, Suffolk, England". In this book a Susan Clutton marries a Samuel Winter, gardener for Lord Huntingfield's estate., and they have a child Elizabeth Winter in Huntingfield (along with all the other children listed here). In this book Samuel and Susan move to Farnworth, Bolton near Manchester in 1836 to work on Thomas Barnes cotton mill and Susan dies circa 1850. I found the following evidence to tie these people in as Betsy Winter's parents: 1) Our Betsy Winter, living in Weston Rhyn, Shropshire, based on numerous census records is born in the region of 1831-1834 (inconstant ages) in Huntingfield, Suffolk (a small townland the other side of the country). 2) On her marriage cert in 1860 she lists her father as a Samuel Winter a farm bailiff (a rare enough profession among the masses) 3) In 1861 census there is a Samuel Winter b 1800 listed as a farm bailiff living only a few fields away from Betsy, in Bronygarth. This Samuel Winter says he is born in Chediston, Suffolk (the other side of the country very very near Huntingfield, Suffolk) and his wife Helena Maria b 1807 is listed as coming from Runcorn, Cheshire. 4) In 1851 census many miles away in Farnworth. Bolton. Lancashire, we have a Samuel Winter b 1798 from Chediston, Suffolk with a wife Helena Maria b 1807 coming from Runcorn, Cheshire with 2 children, Betsy Winter born 1830 in Huntingfield, Suffolk and Lucy Winter born 1831 in Huntingfield, Suffolk 5) In 1841 census still in Farnworth. Bolton. Lancashire, we have a Samuel Winter b 1800 with wife Susan b 1795 with children Samuel 1821, Joseph 1825, Mary 1825, Susan 1826, Betsy 1830, Lucy 1831 all listed as being born in different county. Perhaps a different wife in 1841? Perhaps a leap of faith? But... 6) In Bolton. Lancashire, we have a marriage record between a Samuel Winter and a Helena Maria Wylde listing the marriage as occuring in Jan-March 1845 7) In Bolton. Lancashire we have a death record for a Susan Winter in July-Sep 1844 So it looks like Helena would not have been around in 1841 and Samuel Winter COULD have been married to a Susan in 1841 who had since died (admittedly we have not got as strong a confirmation that this Samuel Winter and Susan are the same family as they do not list their birth places in 1841 census) 8) This is where we step over to the research of the William Clutton tree, which includes: Samuel Winter b 1798 marrying a Susan Clutton b 1791 in 1818 Laxfield, Suffolk (near Chediston and Huntingfield) - Samuel Winter and Susan Clutton having children Samuel 1819, Joseph 1821, Mary 1823, Elizabeth 1824 died young, Susan 1826, Charlotte 1827, Elizabeth 1829, Lucy 1831, Harriet 1834 all born in Huntingfield, Suffolk - Samuel Winter and Susan Clutton moving from Huntingfield, Suffolk in 1836 to the other side of the country Farnworth, Bolton, Lancashire It is from William Clutton's book from which the above ancestry all flows. So... Samuel is born in Chediston, Suffolk and marries Susan Clutton in Laxfield and works as a gardener on Lord Huntingfield's estate in Suffolk where their family is born. In 1836 he moves to Farnworth in Lancashire to work in Thomas Barnes cotton mill. In 1844 his wife Susan dies and in 1845 he remarries Helena Maria Wylde. In 1851 he is listed as a farmers servant living with family at 112 Green Bank, Farnsworth, Lancashire with 2nd wife Helena Maria and the kids, Betsy and Lucy. On Betsy marriage cert he is listed as a farm bailiff an by 1861 he is living with Helena Maria in Bronygarth, Shropshire a few fields away from Betsy and James farm. He died in Shropshire.; [Note Record]

9. Elizabeth Winter (signed her name Betsy on all census forms and certs). She was born in Huntingfield, Halesworth in Suffolk of parents Susan and Samuel Winter. I have reason to believe that this Susan is the same Susan that appears in a genealogical study "William D Clutton, Genealogy and family history of William Clutton (1675-1732) of Fressingfield, Suffolk, England". In this book a Susan Clutton marries a Samuel Winter, gardener for Lord Huntingfields estate., and they have a child Elizabeth Winter in Huntingfield. In this book Samuel and Susan move to Farnworth, Bolton near Manchester in 1836. to work on Thomas Barnes cotton mill and Susan dies in 1850. I found an 1851 census record for Farnworth, which lists a Samuel from Chediston near Huntingfield, Suffolk, with a wife Helena Maria (presumably second wife ). They have with them a child Betsy Winter also from Huntingfield. I also found an 1861 census record which puts the same Samuel Winter and Helena Maria in Bronygarth only a few hundred yards from Betsy Winter and James Richardsons farm. Hence my belief that our Betsy Winter's parents are indeed Samuel Winter and Susan Clutton, from the William Clutton book from which the above ancestry all flows. Betsy Winter ends up in Shropshire, possibly due to the fact that her father's new wife comes from nearby Cheshire. She marries James Richardson in 1860. 12. Ann was born in Daventry, Northamptonshire, where her mother grew up and her Scottish father moved to. Prior to her marriage in 1873 she is living at Bromwick Park, Maesbury, Oswestry and working as a domestic servant. She lived in Maesbury and Maesbury Marsh all her life with George although for some years they did live down the road in the famous St Winifred Wellhouse, Woolston (after Edward moved out). 13. Rebecca Powell Adams was born in Floore/Flore Northamptonshire and was christened in Ravensthorpe. She took the name of her 2 parents. By 1841 she is living with a Frances and Ann Hickerman at Ravensthorpe, Northamptonshire with their son Edward and a William Adams b 1827 presumably her brother. She is listed as a visitor but is also present at the same address 10 years later. In 1851 she is listed as an annuitant, which makes me believe she in now an orphan and possibly of wealthier parents? She meets the Scottish Andrew Currie then living in Daventry and marries in 1852. By 1861 the family have relocated to Shropshire she lives out her days in the Maesbury area. In her 80s she and Andrew moved in with his daughter Ann with her husband George Lewis an their children in Munslow Cottages, Maesbury Marsh.; [Note Record]
14. Mary was born in West Felton in 1815/1816. (Tracing her family was a challenge largely achieved by cousin Valerie Harper based on the following evidence. Her marriage cert cites her father as blacksmith, William Roderick. Her marriage was witnessed by a Hannah Roderick. A widowed midwife, Martha Roderick ended up living next door to her in 1851, the same profession as Mary in later life. We also find a William Roderick and Martha Roderick having 6 other children in West Felton with a gap around the years of Mary's birth. Furthermore this William Roderick has a census record citing him as blacksmith also. Furthermore all the other names of this family show up again in Mary children. This all leads us to believe her parents were indeed this same William Roderick and Martha Roderick for whom we have christening notices for many other children but sadly not including Mary) On 20 Mar 1836 she has an illegit son William Roderick with father Thomas Woollam. After this in 1837 she marries local labourer David Lewis while living in Knockin and working as a servant. They marry at Knockin parish church. They lived mostly in the Maesbury area until moving to Oswestry in 1881 where they lived with her grand daughter Fanny Morgan and a grandson Thomas Currie. Here Mary worked as a midwife. By 1891 she is widowed and living at Dodd's Cottages as a "Monthly Nurse" (a minder post childbirth).; [Note Record]
