Edward LEWIS, eldest son of George LEWIS and Ann CURRIE [Family], was born between 1874 and 04/1874 in Oswestry, Shropshire, England. Edward died about 1945 in Brynycastell Cottage, Gobowen, Oswestry, Shropshire, England aged about 71.

He resided in 1881 in New Bridge, Maesbury, Shropshire, England. He was a Apprentice blacksmith with blacksmith John Thomas in 1891 in Near The Vicarage, St Martins, Shropshire, England. In 1891 Edward resided in Near The Vicarage, St Martins, Shropshire, England. He resided in 1899 in Maesbury, Oswestry, Shropshire, England. Edward resided in 04/1901 in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon, Shropshire, England. Edward resided on 28/03/1902 in Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon, Denbigh, Wrexham, Wales. On 28/03/1902 he was a Railway platelayer in Maesbury, Oswestry, Shropshire, England. On 02/04/1911 Edward resided in 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, Oswestry, Shropshire, England. On 02/04/1911 he was a Railway Playelayer in Gobowen, Shropshire, England. On 22/11/1927 he resided in Brynycastell Cottage, Gobowen, Oswestry, Shropshire, England. In 1935 he resided in Brynycastell Cottage, Gobowen, Oswestry, Shropshire, England.

Born and raised in Maesbury in Shropshire. Edward left home as a teenager and I believe I have found him aged 17 in the 1891 census, working as an apprentice blacksmith and living with a blacksmith family of John Thomas next to the vicarage and Trevor Cottage in St Martins. This is presumably where he met his wife Charlotte Richardson who grew up on a farm in the neighbouring village of Weston Rhyn. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although in later life Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they are living in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close). A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. In 1914 Edward joins the army, although I have found no further information about this. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte dies and for some unknown reason leaves some substantial cash in her will to her brother who at this stage is a farm owner. Edward stays on at Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen after William Owen and Mary Helen started their family there and at one point even shared a room and bed with his grandson Edward Henry.
