Notes

1. Born and reared in Glyn Morlas, Oswestry and worked as a carpenter in Gobowen, Oswestry, Shropshire, England. William lived with his family in Brynycastell Cottage where he raised his 3 children. He was apparently quite fond of his Mary Helen's daudaughteradys (and possibly the reason she came back to stay with the family).; [Note Record]
2. Born in Llanyblodwel at Olde Bryn Cottage, Robert worked as a labourer. After marriage in 1898 in Chirk, he moved to Glyn Morlas, St Martins, Shropshire Road and worked as a horse tender in a coal mine. In 1903 he is listed in coal tiller/filler on Williams birth cert (Possibly Ifton Colliery or Black Park Colliery). His wife Margaret Jane Davies had a father working in the coalmines also, so it is possible that he worked in the mine before 1896 and may have met the Davies here. Margarets grew up beside the Black Park colliery so its likely that they both worked here rather then the nearby Ifron Colliery. By 1911 he was living in 15 Glyn Morlas, St Martins and working as a agricultural labourer (dairy work) Assumed to live at 1 Oswald Place, Oswestry in 1931 (where William lived at the time of his marriage). At this point Robert was working as a gravedigger. In the 30/40s he had moved to 23 Upper Brook Street Oswestry, Shropshire. Later in life he was hit by a motor bike and never fully recovered. In fact it is said that he became paranoid in later life and believed his partner was trying to poisoned him.; [Note Record]
3. John was born and raised in Llanrhaeadr-ym-Mochnant as one of 4 children of farm labourers. In 1858 after marrying Jane Bywater in Bryn, Llanyblodwel he starts a family and initially moves in with Jane's grandparents Edward and Mary Jones, where Jane had been living in Bryn Hall. Here he too worked as a farm labourer. By 1871 they had moved into their own place, Olde Bryn Cottage where they reared their children. He stayed on in Olde Bryn Cottage for the rest of his life but according to 1891 census and Roberts marriage cert in 1898 he ended us as a game keeper. Overall they had 10 children, including a Watkin Watkin :-); [Note Record]
4. William was born in Chirk. Presume he married somewhere around 1862 as that's when the first of 8 children were born. Seems to have worked as a coal miner his whole life in the Chirk/Halton area.; [Note Record]
5. Born and raised in Maesbury in Shropshire. Edward left home as a teenager and I believe I have found him aged 17 in the 1891 census, working as an apprentice blacksmith and living with a blacksmith family of John Thomas next to the vicarage and Trevor Cottage in St Martins. This is presumably where he met his wife Charlotte Richardson who grew up on a farm in the neighbouring village of Weston Rhyn. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although in later life Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they are living in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close) A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. In 1914 Edward joins the army, although I have found no further information about this. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte dies and for some unknown reason leaves some substantial cash in her will to her brother who at this stage is a farm owner. Edward stays on at Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen after William Owen and Mary Helen started their family there and at one point even shared a room and bed with his grandson Edward Henry.; [Note Record]
6. George Lewis was born in Maesbury. Prior to his marriage in 1873 he is listed as a farm labourer still in Maesbury. He lived in Maesbury and Maesbury Marsh all his life although for some years he did live down the road in the famous St Winifred's Wellhouse, Woolston (after Edward moved out). By 1911 he had moved back to Maesbury with his wife younger children and his aged parents in law.; [Note Record]
7. James Richardson is born in Butley between 1834 and 1836, where he was christened in 1836. While his wife Betsy is also from Suffolk it woudl seem from her family movements that she may have made her own way to Shropshire with her family. Certainly she independently moved to near Manchester with her family. A cousin deccended from John Winter Richardson was told a story that a wealthy Shropshire landowner bought horses in Suffolk and commisioned James Richardson to "walk" them to Shropshire and to stay on and train the locals to handle the horses. Whether or not this story is true, he certainly moved to Shropshire, worked there as a groom and became a farmer of large farms in the area, having come from farm labouring parents. He first farmed at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers. He then moved to another farm, and his 2 sons took ovr Trehowel and Tyn-y- Celyn both employing staff. Is has been reported that he "disowned" his youngest, Charlotte, when he became pregnant and later married Edward Lewis (who she may have met at the blacksmth in neighbouring St Martins where he was working as an apprentice). As a widower he retired and moved back in to Tyn-y-Celyin with his eldest son. When he dies he left £630 8s 6d to sons George and John.; [Note Record]
8. In the 1920s Mary Helen gave birth to Gladys, with a previous partner. She lived in the home of her parents at Brynycastle cottage in Gobowen with husband William Owen after their marriage. Later on, daughter Gladys came to live with them, although the rest of the family believed Gladys to be their aunt for many years. In the 1970s, after husband William has passed on, she visited her son Edward Henry's family in Ireland and stayed for some time, after having a stroke, in Ireland. Here she taught her grandson Mark Watkin to read. She would have stayed in Ireland, except for a further stroke after she returned to England making travel difficult. 10. Elizabeth Winter (signed her name Betsy on all census forms and certs). She was born in Huntingfield, Halesworth in Suffolk of parents Susan and Samuel Winter. I have reason to believe that this Susan is the same Susan that appears in a genealogical study "William D Clutton, Genealogy and family history of William Clutton (1675-1732) of Fressingfield, Suffolk, England". In this book a Susan Clutton marries a Samuel Winter, gardener for Lord Huntingfields estate., and they have a child Elizabeth Winter in Huntingfield. In this book Samuel and Susan move to Farnworth, Bolton near Manchester in 1836. to work on Thomas Barnes cotton mill and Susan dies in 1850. I found an 1851 census record for Farnworth, which lists a Samuel from Chediston near Huntingfield, Suffolk, with a wife Helena Maria (presumably second wife ). They have with them a child Betsy Winter also from Huntingfield. I also found an 1861 census record which puts the same Samuel Winter and Helena Maria in Bronygarth only a few hundred yards from Betsy Winter and James Richardsons farm. Hence my belief that our Betsy Winter's parents are indeed Samuel Winter and Susan Clutton, from the William Clutton book from which the above ancestry all flows. Betsy Winter ends up in Shropshire, possibly due to the fact that her father's new wife comes from nearby Cheshire. She marries James Richardson in 1860. They farm at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers. They then moved to another farm, and their 2 sons took over Trehowel and Tyn-y- Celyn both employing staff. Is has been reported that they "disowned" their youngest, Charlotte, when she became pregnant and later married Edward Lewis (who she may have met at the blacksmth in neighbouring St Martins where he was working as an apprentice). Betsy died before her husband at Pentre Bronygarth, Weston Rhyn leaving £136 16s 2d to her husband James.; [Note Record]

She is Christened on April 29 1838 and is raised by her grand parents at Bryn Hall, Llanyblodwel (IGI) She lives at Bryn Hall with the Jones through 1841, 1851 and 1861 census although by 1858 she has married John Watkin who has also moved in to Bryn Hall.
