Notes

1. James Richardson is born in Butley between 1834 and 1836, where he was christened in 1836. While his wife Betsy is also from Suffolk it woudl seem from her family movements that she may have made her own way to Shropshire with her family. Certainly she independently moved to near Manchester with her family. A cousin deccended from John Winter Richardson was told a story that a wealthy Shropshire landowner bought horses in Suffolk and commisioned James Richardson to "walk" them to Shropshire and to stay on and train the locals to handle the horses. Whether or not this story is true, he certainly moved to Shropshire, worked there as a groom and became a farmer of large farms in the area, having come from farm labouring parents. He first farmed at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers. He then moved to another farm, and his 2 sons took ovr Trehowel and Tyn-y- Celyn both employing staff. Is has been reported that he "disowned" his youngest, Charlotte, when he became pregnant and later married Edward Lewis (who she may have met at the blacksmth in neighbouring St Martins where he was working as an apprentice). As a widower he retired and moved back in to Tyn-y-Celyin with his eldest son. When he dies he left £630 8s 6d to sons George and John.; [Note Record]

3. Mary Ellen was born was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time he was 16 in 1881, his family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. She was still living here with her parents in 1891 and she was living with her parents in the same area by 1901. I have found no matching marriage or death records for Mary Ellen after this. She was not present with her father when he was widowed and living with his son in 1911 so she may have married, may have died young or may have just been absent from her brothers home on the night of the census in 1911.; [Note Record]
4. John Winter Richardson was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time he was 14, in 1881, his family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. He married Mary just before the turn of the century and by 1901 he was back managing Trehowel farm himself where he had 2 servants. He presumably spent some time here as the 1911 census and the 1912 Kelly's directory of businesses both place him as farmer at Trehowel. It is unclear whether or not he and the other Richardson's owned these lands or were tenant farmers.; [Note Record]
5. Charlotte Richardson was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time she was 10 in 1881, her family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. In 1891 her future husband Edward was probably working as an apprentice blacksmith in the next village of St Martins, and it is probably here that they met. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although later on Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they lived in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close). A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte died and for some unknown reason leaves £280 7s 6d to her eldest brother, even though he was a successful farmer and Charlotte left a husband and family behind (This amount would have been roughly equivalent to the price of Charlotte's family home at the time); [Note Record]
6. John Winter Richardson was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time he was 15 in 1881, his family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. He married Sarah Ann Rogers around 1893 and by 1901 he was farming Escob Farm (between St Martins and Weston Rhyn) where he had 2 servants. By 1911 he had taken over his fathers farm Tyn-y-Celyn, and his widowed father had come to stay with him, where they now had 3 servants. By 1912 he was still at Tyn-y-Celyn according to the Kelly's directory of businesses. It is unclear whether or not he and the other Richardson's owned these lands or were tenant farmers. His sister Charlotte died in 1927 and for some reason she left £280 7s 6d to him, even though he was a successful farmer and Charlotte left a husband and family behind (This amount would have been roughly equivalent to the price of Charlotte's family home at the time); [Note Record]
