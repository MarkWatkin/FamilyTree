Notes

1. Born and reared in Glyn Morlas, Oswestry and worked as a carpenter in Gobowen, Oswestry, Shropshire, England. William lived with his family in Brynycastell Cottage where he raised his 3 children. He was apparently quite fond of his Mary Helen's daudaughteradys (and possibly the reason she came back to stay with the family).; [Note Record]
2. Born in Llanyblodwel at Olde Bryn Cottage, Robert worked as a labourer. After marriage in 1898 in Chirk, he moved to Glyn Morlas, St Martins, Shropshire Road and worked as a horse tender in a coal mine. In 1903 he is listed in coal tiller/filler on Williams birth cert (Possibly Ifton Colliery or Black Park Colliery). His wife Margaret Jane Davies had a father working in the coalmines also, so it is possible that he worked in the mine before 1896 and may have met the Davies here. Margarets grew up beside the Black Park colliery so its likely that they both worked here rather then the nearby Ifron Colliery. By 1911 he was living in 15 Glyn Morlas, St Martins and working as a agricultural labourer (dairy work) Assumed to live at 1 Oswald Place, Oswestry in 1931 (where William lived at the time of his marriage). At this point Robert was working as a gravedigger. In the 30/40s he had moved to 23 Upper Brook Street Oswestry, Shropshire. Later in life he was hit by a motor bike and never fully recovered. In fact it is said that he became paranoid in later life and believed his partner was trying to poisoned him.; [Note Record]
3. Born and raised in Maesbury in Shropshire. Edward left home as a teenager and I believe I have found him aged 17 in the 1891 census, working as an apprentice blacksmith and living with a blacksmith family of John Thomas next to the vicarage and Trevor Cottage in St Martins. This is presumably where he met his wife Charlotte Richardson who grew up on a farm in the neighbouring village of Weston Rhyn. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although in later life Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they are living in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close) A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. In 1914 Edward joins the army, although I have found no further information about this. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte dies and for some unknown reason leaves some substantial cash in her will to her brother who at this stage is a farm owner. Edward stays on at Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen after William Owen and Mary Helen started their family there and at one point even shared a room and bed with his grandson Edward Henry.; [Note Record]
4. In the 1920s Mary Helen gave birth to Gladys, with a previous partner. She lived in the home of her parents at Brynycastle cottage in Gobowen with husband William Owen after their marriage. Later on, daughter Gladys came to live with them, although the rest of the family believed Gladys to be their aunt for many years. In the 1970s, after husband William has passed on, she visited her son Edward Henry's family in Ireland and stayed for some time, after having a stroke, in Ireland. Here she taught her grandson Mark Watkin to read. She would have stayed in Ireland, except for a further stroke after she returned to England making travel difficult. Mary Helen lived her later years in homes for the elderly in the Gobowen area.; [Note Record]
