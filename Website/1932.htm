Notes

1. In the 1920s Mary Helen gave birth to Gladys, with a previous partner. She lived in the home of her parents at Brynycastle cottage in Gobowen with husband William Owen after their marriage. Later on, daughter Gladys came to live with them, although the rest of the family believed Gladys to be their aunt for many years. In the 1970s, after husband William has passed on, she visited her son Edward Henry's family in Ireland and stayed for some time, after having a stroke, in Ireland. Here she taught her grandson Mark Watkin to read. She would have stayed in Ireland, except for a further stroke after she returned to England making travel difficult. Mary Helen lived her later years in homes for the elderly in the Gobowen area.; [Note Record]
2. Born and reared in Glyn Morlas, Oswestry and worked as a carpenter in Gobowen, Oswestry, Shropshire, England. William lived with his family in Brynycastell Cottage where he raised his 3 children. He was apparently quite fond of his Mary Helen's daudaughteradys (and possibly the reason she came back to stay with the family).; [Note Record]
3. Gladys was born of an earlier partner of Mary Helen and raised as an aunt with the Lewis name. Although she did come to live with her siblings a little later on after mother Mary Helen married William Owen. Gladys married in Gobowen and stayed in the village to this day.; [Note Record]
4. Edward Henry, known as Henry in the UK and Harry in Ireland was born in Gobowen, Oswestry. As a young man he spent some time in the RAF and won scholarships to study at University, but decided against such careers. He met his wife Veronica on a tour or Europe in 1954 and moved to Ireland where he raised a large family, working various careers, including managing his own contract cleaning company. He had a passion for carpentry, building dolls houses and was a keen artist specialising in pencil and pen and ink drawings. He retired to West Cork with Veronica and his daughter Judith's family, where he spends much time in the garden.; [Note Record]
