1. John Richardson is born in Butley where he lives out his days as an agricultural labourer. Although James marriage cert lists him as a farm bailiff. Perhaps this is an exaggeration driven by his new wife's father's similar status, as John lists himself as a labourer through all census records before and after their marriage.; [Note Record]
2. James Richardson is born in Butley between 1834 and 1836, where he was christened in 1836. While his wife Betsy is also from Suffolk it woudl seem from her family movements that she may have made her own way to Shropshire with her family. Certainly she independently moved to near Manchester with her family. A cousin deccended from John Winter Richardson was told a story that a wealthy Shropshire landowner bought horses in Suffolk and commisioned James Richardson to "walk" them to Shropshire and to stay on and train the locals to handle the horses. Whether or not this story is true, he certainly moved to Shropshire, worked there as a groom and became a farmer of large farms in the area, having come from farm labouring parents. He first farmed at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers. He then moved to another farm, and his 2 sons took ovr Trehowel and Tyn-y- Celyn both employing staff. Is has been reported that he "disowned" his youngest, Charlotte, when he became pregnant and later married Edward Lewis (who she may have met at the blacksmth in neighbouring St Martins where he was working as an apprentice). As a widower he retired and moved back in to Tyn-y-Celyin with his eldest son. When he dies he left £630 8s 6d to sons George and John.; [Note Record]
3. Elizabeth Winter (signed her name Betsy on all census forms and certs). She was born in Huntingfield, Halesworth in Suffolk of parents Susan and Samuel Winter. I have reason to believe that this Susan is the same Susan that appears in a genealogical study "William D Clutton, Genealogy and family history of William Clutton (1675-1732) of Fressingfield, Suffolk, England". In this book a Susan Clutton marries a Samuel Winter, gardener for Lord Huntingfields estate., and they have a child Elizabeth Winter in Huntingfield. In this book Samuel and Susan move to Farnworth, Bolton near Manchester in 1836. to work on Thomas Barnes cotton mill and Susan dies in 1850. I found an 1851 census record for Farnworth, which lists a Samuel from Chediston near Huntingfield, Suffolk, with a wife Helena Maria (presumably second wife ). They have with them a child Betsy Winter also from Huntingfield. I also found an 1861 census record which puts the same Samuel Winter and Helena Maria in Bronygarth only a few hundred yards from Betsy Winter and James Richardsons farm. Hence my belief that our Betsy Winter's parents are indeed Samuel Winter and Susan Clutton, from the William Clutton book from which the above ancestry all flows. Betsy Winter ends up in Shropshire, possibly due to the fact that her father's new wife comes from nearby Cheshire. She marries James Richardson in 1860. They farm at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers. They then moved to another farm, and their 2 sons took over Trehowel and Tyn-y- Celyn both employing staff. Is has been reported that they "disowned" their youngest, Charlotte, when she became pregnant and later married Edward Lewis (who she may have met at the blacksmth in neighbouring St Martins where he was working as an apprentice). Betsy died before her husband at Pentre Bronygarth, Weston Rhyn leaving £136 16s 2d to her husband James.; [Note Record]

6. John Winter Richardson was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time he was 14, in 1881, his family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. He married Mary just before the turn of the century and by 1901 he was back managing Trehowel farm himself where he had 2 servants. He presumably spent some time here as the 1911 census and the 1912 Kelly's directory of businesses both place him as farmer at Trehowel. It is unclear whether or not he and the other Richardson's owned these lands or were tenant farmers.; [Note Record]
7. Charlotte Richardson was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time she was 10 in 1881, her family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. In 1891 her future husband Edward was probably working as an apprentice blacksmith in the next village of St Martins, and it is probably here that they met. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although later on Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they lived in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close). A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte died and for some unknown reason leaves £280 7s 6d to her eldest brother, even though he was a successful farmer and Charlotte left a husband and family behind (This amount would have been roughly equivalent to the price of Charlotte's family home at the time); [Note Record]

13. Edward Henry, known as Henry in the UK and Harry in Ireland was born in Gobowen, Oswestry. As a young man he spent some time in the RAF and won scholarships to study at University, but decided against such careers. He met his wife Veronica on a tour or Europe in 1954 and moved to Ireland where he raised a large family, working various careers, including managing his own contract cleaning company. He had a passion for carpentry, building dolls houses and was a keen artist specialising in pencil and pen and ink drawings. He retired to West Cork with Veronica and his daughter Judith's family, where he spends much time in the garden.; [Note Record]
