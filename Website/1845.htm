4. Elizabeth was born in Earl Soham, Suffolk, but when she was about 4 years old, her family moved some miles away to Laxfield, Suffolk. She was the eldest of 13 children. It is here that she probably met her husband Jonathan who was born in Laxfield, 9 years her elder. They may therefore have known each other from quite a young age - their first child was born when Elizabeth was only 18. They presumably set up their first home a few miles away in Mendham as this is where their first 9 children were born. They then had another 8 children in Laxfield, so Elizabeth had 17 children in all over a period of 20 years (although at least 5 died as infants). She lived on a further 20 years so she would have seen all the surviving children to adulthood.; [Note Record]
5. Jonathan was born in Laxfield, Suffolk. His wife-to-be moved there at the age of 4, so they may have met quite young - their first child was born when Elizabeth was only 18. They presumably set up their first home a few miles away in Mendham as this is where their first 9 children were born. They then had another 8 children in Laxfield, 17 children in all over a period of 20 years (although at least 5 died as infants). He lived on a further 30 years so would have seen all the surviving children to adulthood. Jonathan died on Lord Huntingfield's estate at the home of his daughter Susan, where Susan's husband worked.; [Note Record]

I have reason to believe that this Susan is the same Susan that appears in a genealogical study "William D Clutton, Genealogy and family history of William Clutton (1675-1732) of Fressingfield, Suffolk, England". In this book a Susan Clutton marries a Samuel Winter, gardener for Lord Huntingfields estate., and they have a child Elizabeth Winter in Huntingfield. In this book Samuel and Susan move to Farnworth, Bolton near Manchester in 1836. to work on Thomas Barnes cotton mill and Susan dies in 1850. I found an 1851 census record for Farnworth, which lists a Samuel from Chediston near Huntingfield, Suffolk, with a wife Helena Maria (presumably second wife ). They have with them a child Betsy Winter also from Huntingfield. I also found an 1861 census record which puts the same Samuel Winter and Helena Maria in Bronygarth only a few hundred yards from Betsy Winter and James Richardsons farm. Hence my belief that our Betsy Winter's parents are indeed Samuel Winter and Susan Clutton, from the William Clutton book from which the above ancestry all flows. Betsy Winter ends up in Shropshire, possibly due to the fact that her father's new wife comes from nearby Cheshire. She marries James Richardson in 1860. They farm at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers. They then moved to another farm, and their 2 sons took over Trehowel and Tyn-y- Celyn both employing staff. Is has been reported that they "disowned" their youngest, Charlotte, when she became pregnant and later married Edward Lewis (who she may have met at the blacksmth in neighbouring St Martins where he was working as an apprentice). Betsy died before her husband at Pentre Bronygarth, Weston Rhyn leaving £136 16s 2d to her husband James.; [Note Record]

10. James Richardson is born in Butley between 1834 and 1836, where he was christened in 1836. While his wife Betsy is also from Suffolk it woudl seem from her family movements that she may have made her own way to Shropshire with her family. Certainly she independently moved to near Manchester with her family. A cousin deccended from John Winter Richardson was told a story that a wealthy Shropshire landowner bought horses in Suffolk and commisioned James Richardson to "walk" them to Shropshire and to stay on and train the locals to handle the horses. Whether or not this story is true, he certainly moved to Shropshire, worked there as a groom and became a farmer of large farms in the area, having come from farm labouring parents. He first farmed at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers.
