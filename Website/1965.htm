1. Andrew Currie was born in Stewarton, East Ayrshire in Scotland where he was christened some months later. He moved to Daventry in Northamptonshire in the 1840s where he met his future wife Rebecca Powell Adams. He worked as various jobs over his life (Tea trader, rag and bone man, pedlar, insurance agent). By 1861 he had moved to Shropshire with his family where he lived out the rest of his life. In his 80s he and his wife moved in with his daughter Ann with her husband George Lewis an their children in Munslow Cottages, Maesbury Marsh.; [Note Record]
2. Rebecca Powell Adams was born in Floore/Flore Northamptonshire and was christened in Ravensthorpe. She took the name of her 2 parents. By 1841 she is living with a Frances and Ann Hickerman at Ravensthorpe, Northamptonshire with their son Edward and a William Adams b 1827 presumably her brother. She is listed as a visitor but is also present at the same address 10 years later. In 1851 she is listed as an annuitant, which makes me believe she in now an orphan and possibly of wealthier parents? She meets the Scottish Andrew Currie then living in Daventry and marries in 1852. By 1861 the family have relocated to Shropshire she lives out her days in the Maesbury area. In her 80s she and Andrew moved in with his daughter Ann with her husband George Lewis an their children in Munslow Cottages, Maesbury Marsh.; [Note Record]
3. Ann was born in Daventry, Northamptonshire, where her mother grew up and her Scottish father moved to. Prior to her marriage in 1873 she is living at Bromwick Park, Maesbury, Oswestry and working as a domestic servant. She lived in Maesbury and Maesbury Marsh all her life with George although for some years they did live down the road in the famous St Winifred Wellhouse, Woolston (after Edward moved out). By 1911 they had moved back to Maesbury with George their younger children and her aged parents came to stay with them. She died in the Oswestry district.; [Note Record]
4. George Lewis was born in Maesbury. Prior to his marriage in 1873 he is listed as a farm labourer still in Maesbury. He lived in Maesbury and Maesbury Marsh all his life although for some years he did live down the road in the famous St Winifred's Wellhouse, Woolston (after Edward moved out). By 1911 he had moved back to Maesbury with his wife younger children and his aged parents in law.; [Note Record]
5. Alice was born out of Wedlock while Ann still living with parents . In 1871 she is living with her grandparents after Ann has married and moved on. There is no record of her after this.; [Note Record]
6. Born and raised in Maesbury in Shropshire. Edward left home as a teenager and I believe I have found him aged 17 in the 1891 census, working as an apprentice blacksmith and living with a blacksmith family of John Thomas next to the vicarage and Trevor Cottage in St Martins. This is presumably where he met his wife Charlotte Richardson who grew up on a farm in the neighbouring village of Weston Rhyn. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although in later life Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they are living in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close) A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. In 1914 Edward joins the army, although I have found no further information about this. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte dies and for some unknown reason leaves some substantial cash in her will to her brother who at this stage is a farm owner. Edward stays on at Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen after William Owen and Mary Helen started their family there and at one point even shared a room and bed with his grandson Edward Henry.; [Note Record]
