Notes

1. Not much is known of Gladys Maud. She was unknown to sister, Mary Helen's descendents, except for her discovery in the 1911 census; [Note Record]
2. Born and raised in Maesbury in Shropshire. Edward left home as a teenager and I believe I have found him aged 17 in the 1891 census, working as an apprentice blacksmith and living with a blacksmith family of John Thomas next to the vicarage and Trevor Cottage in St Martins. This is presumably where he met his wife Charlotte Richardson who grew up on a farm in the neighbouring village of Weston Rhyn. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although in later life Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they are living in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close) A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. In 1914 Edward joins the army, although I have found no further information about this. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte dies and for some unknown reason leaves some substantial cash in her will to her brother who at this stage is a farm owner. Edward stays on at Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen after William Owen and Mary Helen started their family there and at one point even shared a room and bed with his grandson Edward Henry.; [Note Record]
3. Charlotte Richardson was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time she was 10 in 1881, her family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. In 1891 her future husband Edward was probably working as an apprentice blacksmith in the next village of St Martins, and it is probably here that they met. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although later on Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they lived in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close). A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte died and for some unknown reason leaves £280 7s 6d to her eldest brother, even though he was a successful farmer and Charlotte left a husband and family behind (This amount would have been roughly equivalent to the price of Charlotte's family home at the time); [Note Record]
4. Edward was the love child of Charlotte and Edward, conceived a few months before they married. He worked as a guardsmen in the army. Later in life he ended up in a mental hospital in Shrewsbury; [Note Record]
5. In the 1920s Mary Helen gave birth to Gladys, with a previous partner. She lived in the home of her parents at Brynycastle cottage in Gobowen with husband William Owen after their marriage. Later on, daughter Gladys came to live with them, although the rest of the family believed Gladys to be their aunt for many years. In the 1970s, after husband William has passed on, she visited her son Edward Henry's family in Ireland and stayed for some time, after having a stroke, in Ireland. Here she taught her grandson Mark Watkin to read. She would have stayed in Ireland, except for a further stroke after she returned to England making travel difficult. Mary Helen lived her later years in homes for the elderly in the Gobowen area.; [Note Record]
6. Later in life Andrew lived in Weston Rhyn. He also worked as a truck driver with a transport company in Gobowen; [Note Record]
