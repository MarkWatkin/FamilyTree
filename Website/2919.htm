3. Elizabeth was born in Earl Soham, Suffolk, but when she was about 4 years old, her family moved some miles away to Laxfield, Suffolk. She was the eldest of 13 children. It is here that she probably met her husband Jonathan who was born in Laxfield, 9 years her elder. They may therefore have known each other from quite a young age - their first child was born when Elizabeth was only 18. They presumably set up their first home a few miles away in Mendham as this is where their first 9 children were born. They then had another 8 children in Laxfield, so Elizabeth had 17 children in all over a period of 20 years (although at least 5 died as infants). She lived on a further 20 years so she would have seen all the surviving children to adulthood.; [Note Record]

11. John Winter Richardson was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time he was 14, in 1881, his family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. He married Mary just before the turn of the century and by 1901 he was back managing Trehowel farm himself where he had 2 servants. It is unclear whether or not he and the other Richardson's owned these lands or were tenant farmers.; [Note Record]
12. Charlotte Richardson was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time she was 10 in 1881, her family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. In 1891 her future husband Edward was probably working as an apprentice blacksmith in the next village of St Martins, and it is probably here that they met. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although later on Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they lived in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close). A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte died and for some unknown reason leaves £280 7s 6d to her eldest brother, even though he was a successful farmer and Charlotte left a husband and family behind (This amount would have been roughly equivalent to the price of Charlotte's family home at the time); [Note Record]

14. John Winter Richardson was born at the family farm, Trehowel in Weston Rhyn/St Martins. By the time he was 15 in 1881, his family moved to the larger Tyn-y-Celyn farm. He married Sarah Ann Rogers around 1893 and by 1901 he was farming Escob Farm (between St Martins and Weston Rhyn) where he had 2 servants. By 1911 he had taken over his fathers farm Tyn-y-Celyn, and his widowed father had come to stay with him, where they now had 3 servants. By 1912 he was still at Tyn-y-Celyn according to the Kelly's directory of businesses. It is unclear whether or not he and the other Richardson's owned these lands or were tenant farmers.
