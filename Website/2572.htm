Notes

1. Born in Llanyblodwel at Olde Bryn Cottage, Robert worked as a labourer. After marriage in 1898 in Chirk, he moved to Glyn Morlas, St Martins, Shropshire Road and worked as a horse tender in a coal mine. In 1903 he is listed in coal tiller/filler on Williams birth cert (Possibly Ifton Colliery or Black Park Colliery). His wife Margaret Jane Davies had a father working in the coalmines also, so it is possible that he worked in the mine before 1896 and may have met the Davies here. Margarets grew up beside the Black Park colliery so its likely that they both worked here rather then the nearby Ifron Colliery. By 1911 he was living in 15 Glyn Morlas, St Martins and working as a agricultural labourer (dairy work) Assumed to live at 1 Oswald Place, Oswestry in 1931 (where William lived at the time of his marriage). At this point Robert was working as a gravedigger. In the 30/40s he had moved to 23 Upper Brook Street Oswestry, Shropshire. Later in life he was hit by a motor bike and never fully recovered. In fact it is said that he became paranoid in later life and believed his partner was trying to poisoned him.; [Note Record]
2. Margaret Jane was born and raised in Chirk from a coal mining family. Lived in Halton until she got married in 1898 when she moved to Glyn Morlas with husband Robert Henry where she raised her family; [Note Record]
3. Later in life George lived in Liverpool; [Note Record]
4. As an adult Edward lived in Oswestry. He was the latest surviving sibling of the family; [Note Record]
5. Married to a chicken farmer and lived in Vancouver Island, Canada; [Note Record]
6. Born and reared in Glyn Morlas, Oswestry and worked as a carpenter in Gobowen, Oswestry, Shropshire, England. William lived with his family in Brynycastell Cottage where he raised his 3 children. He was apparently quite fond of his Mary Helen's daudaughteradys (and possibly the reason she came back to stay with the family).; [Note Record]
7. In the 1920s Mary Helen gave birth to Gladys, with a previous partner. She lived in the home of her parents at Brynycastle cottage in Gobowen with husband William Owen after their marriage. Later on, daughter Gladys came to live with them, although the rest of the family believed Gladys to be their aunt for many years. In the 1970s, after husband William has passed on, she visited her son Edward Henry's family in Ireland and stayed for some time, after having a stroke, in Ireland. Here she taught her grandson Mark Watkin to read. She would have stayed in Ireland, except for a further stroke after she returned to England making travel difficult. Mary Helen lived her later years in homes for the elderly in the Gobowen area.; [Note Record]
8. Gladys was born of an earlier partner of Mary Helen and raised as an aunt with the Lewis name. Although she did come to live with her siblings a little later on after mother Mary Helen married William Owen. Gladys married in Gobowen and stayed in the village to this day.; [Note Record]
9. Was in the Army; [Note Record]
10. Edward Henry, known as Henry in the UK and Harry in Ireland was born in Gobowen, Oswestry. As a young man he spent some time in the RAF and won scholarships to study at University, but decided against such careers. He met his wife Veronica on a tour or Europe in 1954 and moved to Ireland where he raised a large family, working various careers, including managing his own contract cleaning company. He had a passion for carpentry, building dolls houses and was a keen artist specialising in pencil and pen and ink drawings. He retired to West Cork with Veronica and his daughter Judith's family, where he spends much time in the garden.; [Note Record]
11. Veronica grew up in Booterstown on the Rock Road with her 2 sisters. She enjoyed long cycles with her father Arthur around Wicklow and was a great fan of opera music. She met her husband Harry on European tour with her sister in 1954. She settled in Dublin where she raised a family of 8 children with Harry (6 of whom were born at home). She retired with Harry to West Cork with Harry and her daughter, Judith's family, where she enjoys the country life in the village of Skibbereen.; [Note Record]
