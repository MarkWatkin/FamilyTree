3. Born and raised in Maesbury in Shropshire. Edward left home as a teenager and I believe I have found him aged 17 in the 1891 census, working as an apprentice blacksmith and living with a blacksmith family of John Thomas next to the vicarage and Trevor Cottage in St Martins. This is presumably where he met his wife Charlotte Richardson who grew up on a farm in the neighbouring village of Weston Rhyn. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although in later life Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they are living in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close) A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. In 1914 Edward joins the army, although I have found no further information about this. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte dies and for some unknown reason leaves some substantial cash in her will to her brother who at this stage is a farm owner. Edward stays on at Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen after William Owen and Mary Helen started their family there and at one point even shared a room and bed with his grandson Edward Henry.; [Note Record]
4. George Lewis was born in Maesbury. Prior to his marriage in 1873 he is listed as a farm labourer still in Maesbury. By 1911 he had moved back to Maesbury with his wife younger children and his aged parents in law.; [Note Record]
5. James Richardson is born in Butley between 1834 and 1836, where he was christened in 1836. A cousin deccended from John Winter Richardson was told a story that a wealthy Shropshire landowner bought horses in Suffolk and commisioned James Richardson to "walk" them to Shropshire and to stay on and train the locals to handle the horses. 7. Elizabeth Winter (signed her name Betsy on all census forms and certs). She was born in Huntingfield, Halesworth in Suffolk of parents Susan and Samuel Winter. I have reason to believe that this Susan is the same Susan that appears in a genealogical study "William D Clutton, Genealogy and family history of William Clutton (1675-1732) of Fressingfield, Suffolk, England". In this book a Susan Clutton marries a Samuel Winter, gardener for Lord Huntingfields estate., and they have a child Elizabeth Winter in Huntingfield. In this book Samuel and Susan move to Farnworth, Bolton near Manchester in 1836. to work on Thomas Barnes cotton mill and Susan dies in 1850. I found an 1851 census record for Farnworth, which lists a Samuel from Chediston near Huntingfield, Suffolk, with a wife Helena Maria (presumably second wife ). They have with them a child Betsy Winter also from Huntingfield. I also found an 1861 census record which puts the same Samuel Winter and Helena Maria in Bronygarth only a few hundred yards from Betsy Winter and James Richardsons farm. Hence my belief that our Betsy Winter's parents are indeed Samuel Winter and Susan Clutton, from the William Clutton book from which the above ancestry all flows. Betsy Winter ends up in Shropshire, possibly due to the fact that her father's new wife comes from nearby Cheshire. She marries James Richardson in 1860. They farm at Trehowel Farm of 30 acres, then Tyn-y-Celyn farm of 117 acres, and employerd servants and labourers. They then moved to another farm, and their 2 sons took over Trehowel and Tyn-y- Celyn both employing staff. Is has been reported that they "disowned" their youngest, Charlotte, when she became pregnant and later married Edward Lewis (who she may have met at the blacksmth in neighbouring St Martins where he was working as an apprentice). Betsy died before her husband at Pentre Bronygarth, Weston Rhyn leaving £136 16s 2d to her husband James.; [Note Record]
