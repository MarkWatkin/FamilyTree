1. Mary was born in West Felton in 1815/1816. (Tracing her family was a challenge largely achieved by cousin Valerie Harper based on the following evidence. Her marriage cert cites her father as blacksmith, William Roderick. Her marriage was witnessed by a Hannah Roderick. A widowed midwife, Martha Roderick ended up living next door to her in 1851, the same profession as Mary in later life. We also find a William Roderick and Martha Roderick having 6 other children in West Felton with a gap around the years of Mary's birth. Furthermore this William Roderick has a census record citing him as blacksmith also. Furthermore all the other names of this family show up again in Mary children. This all leads us to believe her parents were indeed this same William Roderick and Martha Roderick for whom we have christening notices for many other children but sadly not including Mary) On 20 Mar 1836 she has an illegit son William Roderick with father Thomas Woollam. After this in 1837 she marries local labourer David Lewis while living in Knockin and working as a servant. They marry at Knockin parish church. They lived mostly in the Maesbury area until moving to Oswestry in 1881 where they lived with her grand daughter Fanny Morgan and a grandson Thomas Currie. Here Mary worked as a midwife. By 1891 she is widowed and living at Dodd's Cottages as a "Monthly Nurse" (a minder post childbirth).; [Note Record]
2. David was born in Welshpool. He presumably married some time around 1837 although cannot find a marriage cert. He moved to West Felton where is wife Mary came from. He works all his life as an agricultural labourer around Maesbury area until he moved to Oswestry where he lived with his grand daughter Fanny Morgan and a grandson Thomas Curry.; [Note Record]

By 1911 he had moved back to Maesbury with his wife younger children and his aged parents in law.; [Note Record]

8. Born and raised in Maesbury in Shropshire. Edward left home as a teenager and I believe I have found him aged 17 in the 1891 census, working as an apprentice blacksmith and living with a blacksmith family of John Thomas next to the vicarage and Trevor Cottage in St Martins. This is presumably where he met his wife Charlotte Richardson who grew up on a farm in the neighbouring village of Weston Rhyn. Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. This, and word of mouth evidence suggests that Charlotte's match and/or premarital pregnancy caused friction between her and family and she was "disowned". Although in later life Charlotte's grandchildren did visit the Richardson's farm so the "disowning" was not entire. After the marriage by 1901, they are living in 40 New Hall View, Ruabon with new baby Edward Donald and Edward is employed as Railway platelayer (These houses are gone, replaced by St Michael's Close) A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. In 1914 Edward joins the army, although I have found no further information about this. At some point between 1918 and 1927 they moved into Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen and ultimately passed this house onto daughter Mary Helen, who raised her own family there with William Owen. In 1927, Charlotte dies and for some unknown reason leaves some substantial cash in her will to her brother who at this stage is a farm owner. Edward stays on at Brynycastle Cottage, Gobowen after William Owen and Mary Helen started their family there and at one point even shared a room and bed with his grandson Edward Henry.; [Note Record]

Edward and Charlotte marry in late 1899 in the Oswestry registry office (about 4 months pregnant with their first child Edward Donald). At the time of the marriage they are both living in Maesbury (Marsh) and the marriage is witnessed by Edward's parents. A year later, Mary Helen is born at Bringewood, Queen Street, Ruabon and Edward is still listed as a platelayer, as he is in 1911 by which time they have moved to 1 Bryn Castle Terrace, Gobowen, just opposite the school. In 1927, Charlotte died and for some unknown reason leaves £280 7s 6d to her eldest brother, even though he was a successful farmer and Charlotte left a husband and family behind (This amount would have been roughly equivalent to the price of Charlotte's family home at the time); [Note Record]
